TITLE
Haptic Sim-Chair-Based Teleoperation System for Autonomous Vehicle Platforms

AUTHORS
Ryan Simpson, Tyler Carrasco, Yi-Szen Tang, Kevin Xu, Justin Dang, Dr. Behnam Bahr

AFFILIATION (italic)
Autonomous Vehicles Laboratory (AVL), California State Polytechnic University, Pomona (CPP)

============================================================
ABSTRACT (~slightly trimmed, same story as submission)
============================================================

Remote operation of ground vehicles presents fundamental challenges in situational awareness. An operator who is not physically present loses sensory feedback for precise, confident driving, including peripheral vision and the perception of acceleration. Conventional teleoperation systems provide only a flat video feed, limiting use in hazardous environments and autonomy safety intervention.

The Autonomous Vehicles Laboratory (AVL) at California State Polytechnic University, Pomona (CPP) has developed a bidirectional haptic teleoperation system that restores this missing feedback. The test platform carries three ZED X stereo cameras and an Xsens MTi-680G IMU on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin. The operator sits in a D-BOX motion chair with a Thrustmaster wheel and pedals, facing three monitors that recreate a driver's field of view. Steering and pedal inputs go to the vehicle in real time, while measured accelerations drive the chair so the operator feels the forces on the vehicle.

All communication runs over a Tailscale VPN rather than line-of-sight radio, so the operator and vehicle can be any distance apart. This poster presents the system architecture, low-latency video and control over the internet, and applications of immersive teleoperation to autonomous vehicle development.

============================================================
INTRODUCTION
============================================================

Remote drivers lose the cues that make driving intuitive: peripheral vision, body motion, and the feel of acceleration. This project restores them with three channels over Tailscale VPN:

• Vision: panoramic live video from three mounted ZED X cameras
• Control: low-latency wheel and pedal commands to differential drive
• Haptics: IMU accelerations mapped through a washout filter into D-BOX chair motion

============================================================
METHODOLOGY
============================================================

Three UDP links connect the sim-chair station to Marvin's Jetson Orin over Tailscale.

Control (UDP port 5006)
Wheel and pedals send commands to the Jetson, which drives the Teensy and Spark MAX motors. A 300 ms watchdog latches a safe stop if the link drops.

Video (RTP over UDP)
Three ZED X cameras stream H.265 video to three monitors at the chair. Late frames are dropped to keep latency low.

Haptics (UDP port 5007)
The IMU measures kart motion; a washout filter drives the D-BOX chair so the operator feels accel, brake, and cornering. On packet loss, the chair eases to neutral.

Technologies
Jetson Orin · ROS 2 Humble · ZED X (x3) · Xsens MTi-680G · D-BOX LiveMotion · Tailscale · SDL2 · Python / C++

RESULTS (same facts; bold outcome labels — NOT Control/Video/Haptics again)
============================================================

Measurements and demonstration status (sim chair to Jetson over Tailscale):

• Remote driving: end-to-end operation with live three-camera panoramic video
• Pipelines: all three links use UDP (control :5006, video H.265 RTP, haptics :5007)
• Latency: network round-trip typically about 95 to 150 ms on the DERP path; direct LAN used when available for lowest latency
• Video: ZED X to NVENC H.265 to RTP/UDP to three monitors (late frames dropped for low latency)
• Failsafe: control path on UDP :5006 with watchdog-backed stop if the link drops
• Motion cueing: haptic path on UDP :5007 (IMU to washout to D-BOX) so the operator feels vehicle motion in the chair
• Integration: full simultaneous operation of cameras, wheel/pedals, and motion chair

Closing paragraph (paste under the second bullet block / near Fig. 5):

These results confirm that the sim-chair station and Jetson platform can operate as one teleoperation system over Tailscale. The operator receives a live panoramic camera view, sends steering and pedal commands with a watchdog failsafe, and feels vehicle motion through the D-BOX chair. Figures 4 and 5 show the two ends of that loop: the vehicle-side compute and camera feed, and the immersive operator station used for demonstration.


============================================================
CONCLUSION (paragraph)
============================================================

This project shows that consumer simulation hardware and an AVL robotics platform can form a full bidirectional teleoperation loop over the internet. Panoramic video, low-latency control, and washout-based motion cueing restore the sensory feedback remote drivers normally lose with a flat camera feed alone. The same stack supports hazardous-environment operation and autonomy safety intervention, allowing a human to take over with vision and body cues without a line-of-sight radio link. Classical washout on a D-BOX chair, networked video and control, and Tailscale connectivity together give a practical path to immersive remote driving on campus and beyond.
